Getting Started

What DengueTect is, who it's for, and how it fits into your weekly routine.

DengueTect is a dengue forecasting tool for the Marikina City Health Office. You give it the last four weeks of weather and dengue case counts for a barangay, and it estimates how many cases to expect and how likely an outbreak is for the week you choose.

It then shows a risk level (Low, Moderate, or High) with recommended health actions, so your team can prepare before cases peak.

Good to know DengueTect supports your professional judgment. It does not replace it. Always combine the forecast with what you see in the field.

Three barangays along the Marikina River: Nangka, Tumana, and Malanday. These areas flood often, and standing water after heavy rain is where dengue mosquitoes breed.

Other barangays can't be forecast yet, because the models learned only from the case history of these three.

Once a week for each barangay, right after that week's case counts are finalized in your CHO records. Running all three barangays every week keeps the Dashboard and Risk Map up to date.

Tip Pick the same day each week (for example, every Monday morning) so forecasts are easy to compare over time.

Running a Forecast

Entering climate and case data on the Climate Input page.

  1. Go to Climate Input in the menu.
  2. Choose the Barangay and the Week Starting date you want a forecast for.
  3. Click Fetch Climate Data. Rainfall, temperature, and humidity for the past 4 weeks fill in automatically.
  4. Check the values in the Open-Meteo Data Log on the right.
  5. Type the dengue case counts for the past 4 weeks from your CHO records.
  6. Click Run Forecast. The Forecast Report opens when it's done.
Open Climate Input

It downloads daily weather readings for Marikina City from Open-Meteo, a free public weather archive, for the 4 weeks before your chosen date. It then groups the days into weeks:

  • Rainfall is added up for the whole week (mm).
  • Temperature and humidity are averaged over the week.

The Data Log shows exactly which dates went into each week, so you can double-check them.

The weather archive is usually a few days behind. If you pick today or a very recent date, the newest days may not be available yet, and the most recent week can come out too low or even 0.

What to do Pick a date at least a week in the past, then fetch again. You can also type in corrected values yourself. Every field can be edited before you run the forecast.

If you see "Network error," check your internet connection and try again.

Understanding Results

Reading the Forecast Report and deciding what to do next.

The risk level comes from the Outbreak Likelihood percentage on the report:

Lowbelow 50%

Keep routine weekly surveillance, clean-up drives, and breeding-site checks.

Moderate50–74%

Step up surveillance, schedule preventive fogging, and run awareness campaigns.

High75% and up

Activate the response team, prioritize fogging, issue an advisory, and switch to daily reporting.

The full recommended actions for each level are written out in the report's Health Response Recommendations section.

It's how strongly the current weather and case pattern looks like past outbreak weeks in that barangay. In DengueTect, an outbreak week is one where the case count was in the top 25% of all weeks on record for that barangay.

A higher percentage means conditions look more like past outbreaks. It is a warning signal, not a guarantee.

They answer two different questions, and separate models handle each:

Projected Cases"How many?"

An estimate of the number of cases for the week.

Outbreak Likelihood"How unusual?"

Whether that week looks abnormally high for this barangay.

In barangays where most weeks have 0–2 cases, even a few cases can be above normal. So a small number can still come with a High risk level.

Two different prediction models each estimate the case count. The Ensemble combines them (60% Random Forest, 40% XGBoost), because Random Forest performed better in testing.

  • When all three numbers are close, the models agree, and you can rely on the estimate more.
  • When they're far apart, treat the case number with extra caution.

The Dashboard and History pages show the Random Forest estimate.

Terms Explained

Words you'll see around the system, in plain language.

Outbreak week

A week whose case count was in the top 25% of all recorded weeks for that barangay.

Lag

Looking back in time. "Lag 2" means data from 2 weeks before the forecast week.

Rolling average

The average of the last 4 weeks of cases. It smooths out one-off spikes.

Random Forest

A model that asks many simple "decision trees" for a case estimate and averages their answers.

XGBoost

A model that builds decision trees one after another, each fixing the last one's mistakes.

Logistic Regression

The model that turns the data into the Outbreak Likelihood percentage.

Ensemble

A combined estimate: 60% Random Forest + 40% XGBoost.

Open-Meteo

A free, public weather archive the system uses for rainfall, temperature, and humidity.

Aedes aegypti

The mosquito that spreads dengue. It breeds in clean, standing water.
