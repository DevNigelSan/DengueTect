Protect your household

Dengue spreads through the Aedes mosquito, which breeds in clean, standing water. These four habits make the biggest difference.

Search & destroy

Empty, scrub, or cover anything that collects water: pails, old tires, flower vases, bottle caps, and roof gutters.

Protect yourself

Wear long sleeves and pants, use repellent, and sleep under a mosquito net or with screened windows.

Seek early check-up

If someone has a fever for 2 days or more, visit your barangay health center right away. Early care saves lives.

Support community action

Join clean-up drives and allow fogging or spraying when health workers announce it for your area.

Go to a hospital right away if someone with dengue has:

  • Severe stomach pain
  • Vomiting that won't stop
  • Bleeding from the nose or gums
  • Blood in vomit or stool
  • Unusual sleepiness or restlessness
  • Fast or difficult breathing

These can be signs of severe dengue, which needs emergency care, even after the fever goes down.

Questions about this page
Quick answers for residents
What is this page?

It shows this week's dengue risk for Barangays Nangka, Tumana, and Malanday. The Marikina City Health Office makes these forecasts every week using weather and dengue case reports.

What do the colors mean?
LowKeep up weekly clean-ups.
ModerateRisk is rising. Clean up and protect yourself now.
HighAn outbreak is likely. Take extra precautions today.
How often is this updated?

Every week. Check the "Week of" date at the bottom of each card. If a card says the alert is old, ask your barangay health center for the latest advice.

My barangay says "No Forecast Yet."

The health office hasn't published a forecast for your barangay yet. Please check again in a few days.

Is the forecast always right?

No. It is an estimate based on weather and past cases. Always follow official advisories from the health office and your barangay, even when the risk shows Low.

Someone in my family has a fever. What should I do?

If the fever lasts 2 days or more, visit your barangay health center. Go to a hospital right away if you see any danger signs.
